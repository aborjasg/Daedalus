# Compensation Workflows

## Purpose and status

A compensation workflow handles a multi-step operation when later work cannot
complete after earlier steps have durably changed state. Compensation is a
new, owner-authorized business action that addresses an earlier effect; it
is not a database rollback across services, a direct edit to another
service's records, or a guarantee that the original action never occurred.

Current `/docs` establish service-owned writes, asynchronous coordination
through APIs and versioned events, idempotent consumers, outbox/inbox
reliability, bounded retries, and dead-letter recovery. They do not define a
generic saga/orchestrator, a compensation framework, or domain-specific
reversal commands and state machines. Define each workflow's owner,
compensating actions, and recovery contract before implementation. Do not
infer a universal coordinator or automatic rollback behavior.

## Core principles

- Each service changes only its own records. A compensating action affecting
  a domain must be performed by that domain's owning service through its
  documented API or command contract.
- A committed service-local transaction is not undone by a later service's
  failure. Other participants act through documented commands/events and
  persist their own effects.
- Compensation is not necessarily the exact inverse of the original
  action. Some effects may be irreversible, externally visible, or require
  an explicit cancellation, refund, adjustment, or manual resolution
  contract.
- Do not compensate until the workflow has established which earlier steps
  durably completed. A timeout, disconnected client, missing response, or
  delayed event does not prove that a step failed.
- Compensation commands and consumers must be idempotent or have a
  documented duplicate-handling strategy. Retrying a compensation must not
  cause repeated side effects.
- Preserve tenant context, resource authorization, auditability, and
  `correlationId` throughout forward and compensating actions.

## Workflow model

For each multi-service workflow, document its forward steps and the
compensation or recovery outcome for every durable side effect:

```text
Accept command
  -> validate and authorize
  -> owning service commits its local effect
  -> publish documented event after durable commit
  -> participating service applies its own effect
  -> continue or complete workflow
  -> on known failure: retry if safe, compensate through each owner,
     or enter an explicit pending/manual-recovery state
```

This is a logical outline only. It does not prescribe a centralized saga
coordinator, event names, workflow states, timeout values, or a universal
compensation order.

For each step, the workflow contract should distinguish:

1. **Not attempted:** no command was accepted and no durable effect is
   known to have occurred.
2. **In progress or outcome unknown:** the request may have committed; query
   or reconcile with the owning service before retrying or compensating.
3. **Committed:** the owner confirms the effect; continue or compensate
   through that owner as required.
4. **Compensated:** the owner confirms the defined compensating action; this
   is a new state, not erasure of the original audit history.
5. **Failed or requires intervention:** automated recovery cannot safely
   proceed; surface the documented status and alert the responsible
   operator.

Use only states that the owning workflow contract defines. Do not report
success or final failure while a required effect is ambiguous.

## Compensation and recovery choices

When a step fails, the workflow owner classifies the outcome and chooses
among these contract-defined options:

- **Bounded retry:** retry only a transient failure when the operation is
  safe or idempotent, within the documented deadline and retry budget. See
  [05.1-Retry policies.md](./05.1-Retry%20policies.md).
- **Reconciliation:** query the owning service or external provider through
  a documented status/reference contract to determine whether the prior
  action committed. Do not infer the result from a client timeout.
- **Compensation:** issue a documented command to the owner of the effect.
  Track its result independently and make retries safe.
- **Continue with a documented degraded outcome:** only when the workflow
  contract explicitly permits it and records which steps are incomplete.
  Do not convert missing work into a success-shaped response.
- **Manual recovery:** stop automated attempts when action is ambiguous,
  unsafe, or not defined. Alert an authorized operator and preserve
  sufficient safe context for controlled resolution.

If a compensation itself fails, do not bypass the owner or repeatedly
reverse state. Retry only under its policy, reconcile the outcome, and
escalate to the documented pending/manual-recovery path.

## Coordination, persistence, and messaging

- Each service commits its own state in a local transaction. Use an outbox
  when its committed change must reliably publish an event. Consumers use
  durable idempotency/inbox state and acknowledge only after their own
  required effect is durable.
- Events are delivered at least once. Consumers validate the event
  envelope, producer, type/version, tenant context, and preconditions
  before acting. A fact event is not automatically a compensation command.
- A coordinator, if explicitly chosen for a particular workflow, tracks
  workflow progress and sends only documented commands to the relevant
  owners. It does not access their databases or commit a distributed
  transaction.
- Record enough workflow progress to resume safely after a process restart,
  duplicate event, timeout, or dead-letter replay. Define deduplication
  scope, retention, event ordering assumptions, and reconciliation behavior
  in the workflow contract.
- Compensation must not erase the original events or audit facts. Record
  the compensating action and link it to the original workflow/action using
  documented references.
- Dead-letter replay must re-enter normal validation, authorization,
  idempotency, and workflow state checks. See
  [02.3-Dead‑letter strategy.md](../02-Event%20Flow%20%26%20Messaging/02.3-Dead%E2%80%91letter%20strategy.md).

## Domain ownership examples

- **Quote and booking:** Quote Estimator owns quote results. Bookings owns
  booking lifecycle and the accepted booking-price snapshot. If quote
  validation or booking creation fails ambiguously, reconcile with the
  relevant owner. Any cancellation or quote-state transition must use a
  defined owner-controlled contract; no direct cross-schema edit is
  permitted.
- **Booking and scheduling:** Bookings owns booking state; Scheduling owns
  availability and assignments. A later scheduling failure does not allow
  Scheduling to delete or rewrite a booking. Any booking cancellation or
  rescheduling compensation must be requested through a Bookings-owned
  contract, with corresponding Scheduling effects handled by their owner.
- **Job completion and payment:** Jobs owns completion evidence. Payments
  owns payment intents, transactions, refunds, and reconciliation. A
  payment failure does not authorize Payments to undo Job state. Any retry,
  payment adjustment, refund, or job follow-up must follow the respective
  service's documented contract and reconcile provider outcomes.
- **Notifications:** Notifications owns delivery attempts and status.
  A message already delivered cannot necessarily be withdrawn; any
  correction or follow-up notification is a separate owner-authorized
  action, if the contract supports it.

These are ownership examples, not approved compensation endpoints, event
payloads, lifecycle states, or guarantees that a reversal is possible.

## Security, errors, and observability

- Every participant authenticates and authorizes its own action. A
  coordinator's ability to start a workflow does not confer access to
  another service's records or permit it to impersonate a tenant user.
- Resolve tenant scope from trusted authenticated context; API/event
  contracts use `tenantId`, while tenant-scoped persistence uses
  `tenant_id`. Never broaden scope on retry or recovery.
- Return workflow status and errors only as defined by the owning API
  contract and canonical envelope. Include `correlationId`; do not expose
  tokens, secret values, SQL, internal hostnames, raw provider errors, or
  another tenant's resource existence.
- Emit safe telemetry for workflow ID/reference, step, owner, outcome,
  retry/compensation attempt, elapsed time, and `correlationId`. Do not
  place sensitive payloads or credentials in logs or dead-letter
  diagnostics.
- Define alerting and operator permissions for workflows that remain
  pending, compensation fails, reconciliation is inconclusive, or a
  dead-letter requires action.

## Contract checklist

Before implementing a compensating workflow, document:

1. The workflow owner, participants, initiating command, and durable
   forward effects.
2. The state machine and how each step's committed, failed, or ambiguous
   outcome is established.
3. Owner-controlled commands/events for each forward and compensating
   effect, with authorization, tenant scope, versioning, and error behavior.
4. Which effects are reversible, compensatable, retryable, or require
   manual recovery; do not assume every effect has an inverse.
5. Idempotency, outbox/inbox, deduplication, ordering, timeout, retry, and
   dead-letter/replay behavior.
6. User-visible status, partial/degraded outcome rules, audit references,
   monitoring, escalation, and operator permissions.
7. Confirmation that no step depends on cross-schema writes or a
   distributed database transaction.
