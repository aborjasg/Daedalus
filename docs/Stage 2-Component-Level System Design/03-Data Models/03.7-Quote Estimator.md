# Data Model: Quote Estimator

## Purpose and status

The Quote Estimator calculates an indicative price for a requested service
from tenant-defined pricing rules and customer-provided job details. Its
estimate should make the assumed scope and price components understandable
before a customer submits or confirms a booking.

`quotes-api` is the canonical Quote Estimator service, with its own `quote`
PostgreSQL schema and `/api/v1/quotes/*` Gateway route family. It owns pricing rules,
quote requests, estimate calculation, and quote results. This logical model
does not define all physical table names, endpoint-specific DTOs, an
approved price list, or a final pricing formula.

## Scope and ownership

- A quote is a price estimate for a defined service request; it is not a
  booking, invoice, payment intent, or guarantee of final settlement.
- Quote Estimator owns pricing-rule authoring and storage, estimate
  calculation, and quote results.
- Bookings owns booking requests, lifecycle/status, and the accepted
  booking-price snapshot. The Shell submits a quote reference with the
  booking request; the contract between Quote Estimator and Bookings defines
  revalidation, expiry, and snapshot fields.
- Keep the quote and booking as separate service-owned records. Use opaque
  references and documented APIs or versioned events; do not create
  cross-schema joins or duplicate the authoritative accepted booking price.
- The Control Panel Shell collects inputs and displays Gateway responses. It
  does not calculate authoritative prices, persist quotes, or access service
  tables.
- Payments owns payment intents, transactions, refunds, reconciliation, and
  provider references. It does not own quote calculation or treat an estimate
  as a payment.

## Logical entities

| Entity | Purpose | Ownership/status |
|---|---|---|
| Quote request | Validated description of the service, property, condition, requested schedule/frequency, and add-ons to price. | Quote Estimator accepts and validates the request. |
| Pricing rule set | Versioned configuration for base rates, factors, minimums, adjustments, and effective periods. | Quote Estimator owns the rules; tenant-specific rules are tenant-scoped. |
| Quote estimate | Calculated result for a quote request, with currency, total, itemized components, assumptions, and reference to the rule-set version used. | Quote Estimator calculates and returns the result; persistence and retention are defined by its service contract. |
| Quote line item | Explains a priced service or adjustment, including quantity/unit and amount where applicable. | Quote Estimator defines the output fields and calculation. |
| Booking price snapshot | Captures the quote and pricing terms accepted for a booking so later rule changes do not silently rewrite the agreed price. | Bookings owns the snapshot and booking lifecycle. |

These concepts do not mandate separate tables. Do not create a shared pricing
schema or generic pricing service without an explicit ownership decision.

## Input model

The existing draft identifies the following possible cleaning-estimate
inputs. The owning service must define which are required, optional, bounded,
and applicable to each service type:

| Input group | Candidate inputs | Pricing relevance |
|---|---|---|
| Property characteristics | Square footage, bedrooms, bathrooms, property type, floors/stairs, occupants, pets | Estimate workload, access, and cleaning complexity. |
| Service scope | Service type, requested tasks, included tasks, exclusions, custom requests | Selects the work being estimated and avoids ambiguity about scope. |
| Condition | Condition level, first-time service indicator, clutter or exceptional conditions | Adjusts expected labor and may require manual review. |
| Add-ons | Inside fridge/oven, interior windows, interior cabinets, walls, carpet shampoo, garage sweep | Adds task-specific time, materials, or quantity-based pricing. |
| Scheduling and recurrence | Requested time/window and service frequency, where supported | May affect availability or an explicitly approved recurrence adjustment. |
| Location and travel | Service location or a derived travel band, if supported | May affect travel charges; exact location handling is not defined. |

Inputs must be validated by the owning backend. Client-calculated hours,
totals, discounts, or tenant identifiers are not authoritative. Keep
presentation labels and form state separate from the persisted input
contract. Collect only information needed to estimate the requested service.

## Pricing rules and calculation

The source draft proposes workload factors and cost components, including
base labor, service-type and condition multipliers, add-on time or unit
charges, minimum fees, recurrence discounts, travel, supplies, overhead, and
margin. These are candidate rule categories, not approved rates or a complete
calculation. The example rates and percentage ranges in the draft must not be
treated as production prices.

The pricing owner must define and version the applicable rules, including:

- Base scope and rate, labor/time assumptions, and any service-specific
  multipliers.
- Supported condition levels and add-ons, with defined units, quantities,
  and rounding behavior.
- Any minimum charge, travel charge, recurrence adjustment, market
  adjustment, overhead allocation, or margin policy.
- Whether tax is included, excluded, or calculated by a separate authority.
- Currency, precision, rounding, effective dates, rule precedence, and
  behavior when rules overlap or required inputs are missing.
- Which inputs or rule combinations trigger manual review rather than an
  automated estimate.

Use deterministic, testable calculations for a given input snapshot and
rule-set version. Persist or otherwise retain enough rule-version and
input-snapshot information to explain a quote that has been issued or
accepted. Internal costs, labor rates, payroll burden, supplier costs,
overhead, target margins, and market-adjustment logic are business-sensitive;
do not disclose them in customer-facing responses unless an explicit product
contract authorizes it.

No exact formula, currency, tax treatment, adjustment range, or production
price is defined by current docs. Do not infer or hard-code one from the
illustrative ranges in the source draft.

## Estimate result and lifecycle

An estimate should logically identify:

- The quote and tenant context, plus a stable correlation/reference value as
  defined by its owner.
- The service and scope priced, a safe summary of the accepted inputs, and
  any assumptions, exclusions, or manual-review requirements.
- The itemized customer-facing charges and final estimated amount, with
  currency and the documented tax treatment.
- The pricing-rule version and calculation time, and an expiry or
  revalidation condition if the owner defines one.
- A lifecycle state sufficient to distinguish an estimate from an accepted,
  expired, superseded, or manually reviewed quote, if those states are
  supported.

Allowed states, retention, expiry, and whether a quote is saved are not
specified by current docs. Define them before exposing an API. If an estimate
is accepted into a booking, Bookings must own the resulting booking state and
the contract must specify which quote terms are copied or referenced. A
subsequent rule change must not silently alter a previously accepted price.
If scope or assumptions change, re-estimation and customer confirmation
behavior must be defined by the service contracts.

## Tenant isolation and privacy

- Tenant-specific pricing rules and saved estimates are isolated by trusted
  tenant context. Tenant-scoped persistence uses `tenant_id`; API and event
  contracts use `tenantId`.
- The Gateway establishes tenant context from the validated identity. The
  owning backend validates it and authorizes access to the quote or pricing
  rule. A caller-provided `tenantId` is not authoritative.
- Every read, update, list, export, and estimate calculation uses the
  authorized tenant context where applicable. An identifier alone does not
  authorize access to a quote from another tenant.
- Shared/global rules are permitted only when their owner documents intended
  visibility and how tenant-specific overrides are applied.
- Minimize retention of customer and property details. Do not put payment
  credentials or provider secrets into quote inputs, outputs, logs, or
  events.
- A quote or booking price does not grant authorization to customer,
  scheduling, payment, or other domain records.

These requirements follow the documented tenant-isolation and service
ownership rules; any physical indexes, constraints, RLS policies, and
retention periods remain to be defined by the owner.

## API and service interaction

The Shell submits quote inputs through the `/api/v1/quotes/*` Gateway route
family and renders Quote Estimator's response. Endpoint-specific paths,
request/response DTOs, field constraints, and HTTP status mappings are not
yet defined; Quote Estimator owns those contracts.

The owning service contract must define authentication, authorization,
request validation, amount representation, estimate response, safe error
behavior, idempotency for retryable commands, and correlation-ID handling.
The service is authoritative for calculation and tenant/resource
authorization. A UI estimate is not final merely because it was displayed or
cached by the Shell.

The booking handoff should use a documented API or versioned event, not a
cross-schema join. The contract must define what identifies the estimate,
which pricing terms Bookings snapshots, and how stale or expired estimates
are handled. Payments creates payment intents from its own documented
workflow; it must not charge from an unconfirmed estimate.

## Contract decisions before implementation

Before implementing quote endpoints and pricing, document:

- The service-owned physical schema, identifiers, constraints, tenant scope,
  and retention for pricing rules and quote results.
- The contract between Quote Estimator and Bookings for quote references,
  revalidation, expiry, accepted-price snapshots, and stale quotes.
- The supported service types and validated input schema, including units,
  limits, optionality, privacy, and manual-review conditions.
- The formula, rates, currency, precision, rounding, tax treatment,
  minimums, discounts, travel, overhead, and market-adjustment policies.
- Quote identifiers, persistence, statuses, expiry, recalculation, retention,
  and concurrency behavior.
- Endpoint paths within `/api/v1/quotes/*`, request/response DTOs, errors,
  idempotency, authorization, and API versioning.
- Tenant isolation, audit requirements, operational ownership, and safe
  treatment of internal cost and margin data.

Until those decisions are documented, treat the inputs and rule categories
above as a logical starting point only; do not treat the draft's example
amounts or percentages as approved customer pricing.
